# Numerical Analysis – Unit 6 Reflection

## What? (Description)

In Unit 6 of Numerical Analysis, I studied statistical inference, confidence intervals, and hypothesis testing. The unit revisited inferential statistics and the normal distribution, building on concepts introduced earlier in the module. I learned how sample data can be used to make conclusions about larger populations and how statistical methods support decision-making under uncertainty.

The unit introduced confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, test statistics, significance levels, and p-values. I examined how researchers use null and alternative hypotheses when investigating claims and how statistical significance is used to determine whether observed results are likely due to chance. The learning materials also explained the relationship between confidence intervals and hypothesis testing and demonstrated how both approaches are used to support evidence-based conclusions.

As part of the unit activities, I reviewed the reading materials, studied confidence intervals and hypothesis testing, completed the Statistics Quiz, participated in the ongoing collaborative discussion from Unit 5, and prepared for the seminar on statistical significance and effect size.

---

## So What? (Interpretation)

This unit demonstrated the importance of statistical inference when making decisions about populations based on sample data. In many real-world situations, collecting information from an entire population is not practical, so inferential statistics provide a structured way to make reliable conclusions while accounting for uncertainty.

As a Senior Application Programmer in the telecommunications industry, I frequently analyse operational data, automation performance metrics, system monitoring information, and service performance reports. Statistical inference is valuable because it provides a framework for determining whether observed improvements or issues are statistically meaningful rather than random fluctuations.

The concepts of confidence intervals and hypothesis testing were particularly important because they provide objective methods for evaluating evidence. Understanding p-values, significance levels, and confidence intervals strengthened my ability to interpret statistical results and critically assess claims supported by data.

The unit also highlighted the role of effect size and statistical significance in decision-making. I learned that statistically significant results are not always practically significant and that effect size should be considered when evaluating the real-world importance of findings.
