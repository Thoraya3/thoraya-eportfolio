## What? (Description)

In Unit 12 of Numerical Analysis, I studied Bayesian Data Analysis and Bayes’ Theorem. The unit introduced Bayesian statistics as an alternative approach to statistical inference and demonstrated how probability can be used to update existing knowledge when new evidence becomes available.

<br>

The unit focused on Bayes’ Theorem, prior probability, likelihood, posterior probability, and the differences between Bayesian and Frequentist statistical approaches. I learned that Bayesian analysis combines prior knowledge with observed data to produce updated probability estimates.

<br>

A key concept introduced during the unit was:

\[
P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)\times P(A)}{P(B)}
\]

<br>

This formula provides a method for updating the probability of an event when additional information becomes available.

<br>

I also learned that Bayesian Data Analysis generally consists of:

- Creating a statistical model.
- Defining prior distributions.
- Using observed data to calculate posterior probabilities.
- Summarising and interpreting posterior distributions.

<br>

The lecture materials explored the strengths, limitations, merits, and challenges of Bayesian methods and compared Bayesian inference with the Frequentist approaches studied throughout the previous units of the module.

<br>

As part of the probability activity, I applied Bayes’ Theorem to a COVID-19 dataset scenario involving recovery rates from different Indian states.

<br>

The unit also introduced the final module assessments, including the Statistical Analysis Presentation and the Individual Reflection.

## So What? (Interpretation)

The introduction to Bayesian statistics provided a different perspective on statistical inference compared with the methods studied throughout most of the module. Previous units focused primarily on Frequentist methods such as confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, p-values, correlation, regression, and significance testing.

<br>

Unit 12 demonstrated that Bayesian statistics approaches uncertainty differently. Instead of treating model parameters as fixed and data as random, Bayesian methods treat the parameters themselves as uncertain and update beliefs using observed evidence.

<br>

One of the most interesting aspects of Bayesian analysis is the incorporation of prior information. This reflects many real-world decision-making situations where historical knowledge, expert judgement, or previous observations already exist before new data becomes available.

<br>

As a Senior Application Programmer working within the telecommunications industry, Bayesian thinking is highly relevant. Operational decisions are often made using both previous experience and newly observed information. For example, if a service issue has historically occurred within a specific application, new monitoring evidence can be combined with historical experience to reassess the likelihood of future incidents.

<br>

The comparison between Bayesian and Frequentist approaches also helped me appreciate that no single statistical method is appropriate for every situation. Different analytical questions may require different approaches depending upon the data, assumptions, and purpose of the analysis.

<br>

The unit further reinforced the connection between probability, statistical inference, decision-making, and Artificial Intelligence. Bayesian methods are widely used in AI systems, probabilistic modelling, and machine learning applications where uncertainty must be managed systematically.

As part of Unit 12, I completed a Bayesian probability activity based on a COVID-19 India reporting scenario.

<br>

- Total reports: 270
- Kerala reports: 52
- Delhi reports: 20
- Reports with recoveries: 55
- Kerala reports with recoveries: 19
- Delhi reports with recoveries: 8

<br>

The activity required calculation of:

- P(Kerala)
- P(Recovery)
- P(Recovery|Kerala)
- P(Recovery|Delhi)
- P(Kerala|Recovery)

<br>

Using Bayes’ Theorem:

\[
P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)\times P(A)}{P(B)}
\]

<br>

## Reflection

One of the most valuable lessons from Unit 12 was understanding that probability can be updated as new information becomes available. This perspective differs significantly from the Frequentist methods studied earlier in the module and provided an alternative way of thinking about uncertainty and evidence.

<br>
