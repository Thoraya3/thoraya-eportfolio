# Numerical Analysis – Unit 4 Reflection

## What? (Description)

In Unit 4 of Numerical Analysis, I studied probability, probability distributions, and sampling methods. The unit introduced probability as a way of measuring uncertainty and understanding the likelihood of events occurring. It explained how probability theory is used to support decision-making when outcomes cannot be predicted with complete certainty.

The unit covered the fundamental rules of probability and demonstrated how probability can be applied in business and real-world situations. I learned about both discrete and continuous probability distributions and explored how these distributions are used to model uncertainty and random events.

The lecture materials introduced the three main discrete probability distributions: Binomial, Poisson, and Hypergeometric distributions. The unit also covered continuous distributions including the Normal, Uniform, Exponential, and Gaussian distributions. In addition, I learned about different sampling techniques including simple random sampling, stratified sampling, systematic sampling, and cluster sampling.

As part of the formative activities, I explored probability simulations using Wolfram Demonstrations, examined probability distribution models, reviewed sampling methods, and completed exercises designed to strengthen my understanding of uncertainty, probability, and statistical decision-making.

## So What? (Interpretation)

This unit helped me understand how uncertainty can be measured and managed using statistical techniques. In real-world decision-making, it is rarely possible to predict outcomes with complete certainty. Probability provides a framework for assessing risk and evaluating likely outcomes using available information.

As a Senior Application Programmer working in the telecommunications industry, I regularly encounter uncertainty when analysing system performance, service quality, automation outcomes, and business processes. Understanding probability distributions allows me to interpret patterns, evaluate risks, and make more informed decisions based on data rather than assumptions.

The study of sampling methods was particularly valuable because most real-world analysis relies on samples rather than entire populations. Understanding how different sampling approaches influence the quality and reliability of conclusions reinforced the importance of collecting representative data. This has direct relevance when analysing customer behaviour, operational performance, and reporting metrics within enterprise environments.

The exploration of probability distributions also strengthened my understanding of how statistical models are used within Artificial Intelligence and Data Science. Many predictive models rely on probability theory and distribution assumptions to estimate future outcomes and support decision-making. The simulations provided practical insight into how random events behave and how probability can be visualised and interpreted.
